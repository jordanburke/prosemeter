I'd push back on this. Deleting the tests fixes the color of the CI badge, not the problem.

A test that fails more than twice a month is telling you one of two things: either it's flaky (bad test, worth fixing or removing), or it's catching a real intermittent bug (good test, doing its job). Deleting on failure count alone doesn't tell them apart — it treats "annoying" and "wrong" as the same thing.

The actual problem, as you've named it, is that the team ignores CI. Deleting tests to force green doesn't fix that habit — it just removes the evidence. The next time someone breaks something those tests would have caught, nobody finds out until production.

**What I'd do instead:**

1. Triage the failing tests one by one, not as a batch. For each: is it flaky (bad setup, timing, shared state, race condition) or is it catching something real?
2. Fix or quarantine the genuinely flaky ones — most CI tools support marking a test as quarantined/skipped-but-tracked, so it stops blocking the build without disappearing from the record.
3. Fix the underlying bugs for the ones catching real failures. Deleting those hides risk instead of removing it.
4. Once the suite is reliably green, treat a red build as blocking, always. That's what actually stops the team from ignoring CI — not a one-time purge.

Green tests are the goal, but the way there is fixing what's broken, not deleting the smoke alarm.
