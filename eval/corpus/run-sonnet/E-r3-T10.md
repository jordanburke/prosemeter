I'd push back on this — it fixes the symptom and hides the disease.

A test that fails more than twice a month is telling you one of two things: either it's flaky (bad test, real fix needed) or it's catching a real intermittent bug (good test, real fix needed elsewhere). Deleting it doesn't resolve either case — it just stops you from finding out which one you're looking at. The bug, if there is one, ships to production instead of failing in CI.

The green suite this buys you is fake signal. The whole point of CI going green is "you can trust this." A suite that's green because you removed anything that ever failed isn't trustworthy — it's just quiet.

What I'd do instead:

1. **Quarantine, don't delete.** Move flaky tests to a separate job that runs but doesn't block merges. That stops people from ignoring the main suite without throwing away the test.
2. **Triage each one.** For every test on the "failed twice" list, spend ten minutes asking: is this test flawed (bad setup, timing assumption, shared state), or is it finding something real? That's a fast sort, and it tells you where the actual problem is.
3. **Fix the top offenders, track the rest.** A quarantined test with an owner and a ticket is very different from a deleted test with no record it ever existed.

If the real problem is "the team ignores CI failures," deleting tests won't fix that culture — it'll just remove the evidence that the pattern is real.
